Who I Am

Well, I don't exactly know how to start this, because I don't have anything romantic or fancy behind this website. Nothing like "I want to become the voice of the voiceless" or "I want to become the 4th pillar of democracy." To be honest, I am starting this website so that in future, if I ever get an opportunity to sit in front of a UPSC interview panel, I will have something that I can show them along with my marksheet and degree. Another reason to create this website is to impress at least a few people. Yes, that's why I am starting this, but that isn't the only reason.

When I was thinking about building something, I thought, why not build something that will reflect my real identity and what I actually think about the country which I wish to serve one day, and about the world of which I am a responsible citizen. And somewhere behind it, there was a thought that maybe my writing could be helpful for at least a single person, and a satisfaction that I have something of my own, built by me. I had the option to restart my tabla journey, since I had already completed two stages before the lockdown, but I thought, why not writing instead, since it's something interesting — just typing out what's going on in my mind. So that's why you are seeing this post and this website.

A glimpse of me

So I am a student, an Indian student. I say Indian student because being an Indian student means being in a race — a race of marks, exams, ranks, high paying jobs, and more. But my race wasn't normal like other students'. Yes, I am visually impaired.

My visual impairment is not my identity, it's a small part of it. My identity is way bigger than my physical ability, but the problem with society is that it tries to limit me based on my ability.

Many people love to decide what I can and cannot do, and I love to prove them wrong.

When I was in Class 10th, somebody told me I couldn't do anything. I scored 87.4% that year, and 84.0% two years after. I am not telling you these numbers to flex in front of you — that's something I don't like much, because my story isn't something extraordinary. It's almost normal, with a unique twist in it. The thing is, people create limits for me in their minds, and I cross them.

There are many more things to tell about me, but this is the first, introductory post of the website, not my autobiography, so let's move ahead :-)

Why IAS

Nobody has asked me this question yet, maybe they don't care about it much, but I feel it's a question worth answering even if nobody has asked me.

I don't have a generic, polished answer like the ones people generally expect — social service, changing the system, or something revolutionary. I would rather join politics if I wanted to do that. IAS isn't for big revolutionary changes in the country, but for real, ground-level structural changes in society, and I want to do that. Along with this, I want something else, and that is authority, stability, and power. Yes, you read that correctly, I want power and authority, and I don't think it's wrong to say so publicly. I know some of you would be wondering, or even uncomfortable with that word, and I know why. I know power isn't the reason we're taught to give for entering civil services — or the more correct statement is, we publicly don't pretend it's not a good reason. But I feel something else.

Power isn't necessarily something that should be wrong or misused. Instead, if it's in the right hands, it's the most useful and powerful tool a person can have. And the thing is, thinking deeply or writing an intellectual essay rarely brings change, but power and authority, along with good intentions and a feeling of serving society, can bring real change. So I don't just want to become a writer, but someone who creates change, even at a small level. One more thing worth mentioning here is that I want to create impact. I don't want to become a national hero, but someone who has really improved lives, because people know a national hero, but people remember a person who has improved their life, even if he isn't famous.

To be specific, I want power to create impact in 3 sectors, and they are:

1. **Education**: I personally feel that education is the only thing which can truly liberate a person. It opens the opportunity to become free and independent, and to completely change who you are right now. Even I feel that what I am currently is just because of education, so I would focus on it.

2. **Digitalization**: I will focus on digitalization because I think it's the most important thing in today's world. It's important because if you want to connect with the young generation, you can't ignore it — you can't expect them to stand in lines for hours or visit government offices regularly. Digitalization is the need of the hour, even at the local level.

3. **Accessibility**: When I say accessibility, many of you will think about lifts or accessible walking areas, but it's not limited to this. Accessibility is something way bigger than what people think. It's not just about buildings or parks, but something that everybody must have at a mental level.

It's not that I've mentioned these three things so I will focus only on them and nothing else, but these are my priorities.

I have many more small and big ideas, plans, and initiatives, but I don't want to sound like a party manifesto, so let's move ahead.

Why politics and all

Well honestly, even I don't know why politics, but yes, it is what it is. Actually, I think it's because of the environment in which I grew up — my family was active in local politics, so I think that's the key reason I got interested in it. I don't know how or why, but maybe it's also the reason I was interested in it so early, from childhood, to be specific. You might feel it's an exaggeration, but I once continuously watched news for over 4-5 hours when I was in 9th class, even though I didn't understand much of anything. Yes, it sounds nonsensical, even stupid, but I actually did it, and I don't know why I did that.

But to be serious, I got actively interested in politics in 2019, during the Lok Sabha and Maharashtra Assembly elections. I didn't understand much, but I was watching everything and getting more curious. The most interesting time was the Maharashtra Assembly elections, and I don't know how, but the 2019 Maharashtra Assembly elections were the most dramatic elections I saw. From then I started watching the news, and the turning point was in 2022 — the split in Shiv Sena and the 10-day drama which resulted in the resignation of then Chief Minister Uddhav Thackeray. My 10th board exams were over and I was on holiday, so I was watching everything, and I think that was the time I stopped just watching the news and started understanding it. I think that's the event which changed my views and thinking on politics, and on the society that views it, and, in some way, controls it.

What you can expect

This is the part where I will be extremely honest with you. Don't expect daily or weekly breaking news or updates, I am not here to do that, there are many platforms for that. You will get hardly 3-4 pieces of writing per month, or even less, but it will be more than just news.

Don't expect that I will promote any specific ideology or agenda, that's never going to happen here. You might and should disagree with me, but it should be logical and constructive. Many times I will write something unpopular, or even slightly controversial, but I expect a meaningful debate rather than aggression.

Don't expect me to be neutral. Yes, I am not neutral, and no human on the planet can be. That doesn't mean I support any party or ideology, but it means I have some values through which I observe and think about anything, and it will reflect in my writing. But the important thing is that I will not hide my bias inside any fact. I will explicitly state what is a fact and what its exact source is, and I will explicitly state what my opinion is, and why I think that. And yes, you have an absolute right to disagree with me on anything and everything, and you also have the right to question the sources or my thinking. I feel that you all will, and must, exercise that right, because even I have the right to be proven wrong, and the right to correct myself.

You can expect writing and essays on various topics, including but not limited to:

1. **Politics**: One of my favourite topics. Expect deep dives and essays on current, recent, and historic political events. This will probably be the most interesting part of the website.

2. **Society**: The most thought-provoking, and sometimes uncomfortable, part of the website — questions about Indian society many of us never dared to talk about.

3. **History**: I'm not a historian, so I won't interpret. I'll try to understand what happened, and why it happened the way it did.

4. **International relations**: A subject I love to watch and understand more than build strong opinions on. Critical, fact-based writing with minimum personal views.

5. **Miscellaneous**: Things that don't fall into any category — from a random idea to India's economic policy to how the government can manage traffic in Pune. Literally anything.

So this is all you can, and cannot, expect from me and this website, and I am always open to all kinds of discussion and debate about this.

Why to read me

Well, this is a question even I can't answer, because obviously, if I were in your place, even I wouldn't be reading a random person's writing just after reading his first post, and honestly there's no valid reason to do that. To be honest, you shouldn't read me just because I am preparing for UPSC, that's not a valid reason to read a person. Don't read me because you feel I am intelligent or smart, I am not — I know and can write this only because I am studying this. Read my first 3-4 pieces of writing, and then read me only if you think I am worth your time and energy. Only read me if you feel I am worth reading, and if you feel this guy told you something new or interesting.

So this is all that I think I should tell you before starting the writing. I am starting the website with this post because I thought you should know who you are reading, why I am writing, and why I am writing in the way I am writing it.

And if you are still reading this, then really, thank you, from the bottom of my heart, because writing this really did take effort.

Let's see where this project goes, and where it reaches in the future.

And welcome.
